TogoVar in general

What are the objectives and utilities of TogoVar?

NGS technologies have enabled large-scale genome sequencing to capture variants at low frequencies. However, existing databases do not contain sufficient variant frequency data for Japanese populations. To address this problem, TogoVar seeks to provide a reliable frequency information by assembling variant information from the NBDC Human Database/Japanese Genotype-phenotype Archive (JGA) and public Japanese genomic variation data.

In addition, since the reference human genome sequence can be updated, the genomic position may vary for the same variant if it refers to a different genome build. Thus, in our database, a unique ID is assigned to the same variant, thereby preventing the need to convert the positional information of the variant among different reference sequences, as done by researchers.

About the data collected in TogoVar

Can I retrieve phenotype information on an individual level?

TogoVar database does not keep private information, and we do not currently provide phenotype information for each individual. For more details about phenotype information, please directly contact the research groups described in the JGA-WGS dataset, JGA-WES dataset , and JGA-SNP dataset.

Are the data linked with information about gene expression and proteins?

TogoVar integrates publicly available information on human genomic variants, primarily for the Japanese population. We also provide protein-centered views, including mapping variant positions onto 3D structures fromPDBj/AlphaFold(e.g.BRAF gene) and a Protein Browser that aligns variants with key annotations along the amino acid sequence, such as phosphorylation sites (jPOST), glycosylation sites (GlyCosmos), and disease-associated variants (UniProt) (e.g.BRCA2 gene). Because TogoVar is built on RDF, it can be readily extended to link with gene expression and other RDF-based resources, and we plan to expand these links step by step.

What software is used to detect variants, and how is data quality managed?

The software and quality-control methods differ by dataset. See “Data processing method” for each dataset from theTogoVar datasets page. Details for JGA-WES are provided on theJGA-WES data processing method page.

What are the targeted variant types for TogoVar?

There are five targeted variant types for TogoVar: SNV, Insertion, Deletion, Indel, and Substitution. Of 5 types that HGVS enumerated as the types of variants, Duplication is classified as Insertion, and Indels, for which the lengths of Insertion and Deletion are the same, are classified as Substitution. To identify the type of the variant, Variant Effect Predictor (VEP) is used.

Where does TogoVar obtain consequence annotations?

The value of variant consequence in Variant Effect Predictor (VEP) is displayed. If there are multiple transcripts for a single variant, only the most critical consequence may be displayed.

Where does TogoVar obtain clinical significance annotations?

The value of clinical significance in ClinVar and clinical significance in MGeND are displayed.

Was a liftover tool used for generating variant data?

Yes. Some of the GRCh38 datasets have been created by lifting over their GRCh37 datasets. Please refer to the Liftover GRCh37 column in the TogoVar datasets (GRCh38) pagefor the target datasets. A liftover tool transannohas been used since the release on November 16, 2023. CrossMapwas used until the release on July 14, 2023.

Why do allele frequencies differ between datasets even within the same Japanese ancestry group?

Even within the same Japanese ancestry group, allele frequencies may differ between datasets when participants’ regions of origin or sampling designs differ. Fine-scale genetic structure related to geographic regions has been reported within the Japanese population, and differences in regional composition or participant selection may therefore be reflected as differences in allele frequencies between datasets. Other factors, such as sample size, age distribution, disease status, sequencing conditions, variant calling, quality control, and allele frequency calculation methods, can also contribute to such differences.

Reference:
Sakaue S. et al. Dimensionality reduction reveals fine-scale structure in the Japanese population with consequences for polygenic risk prediction. Nature Communications, 2020.

What is SSCV DB?

SSCV DB is a database of splice-site creating variants (SSCVs) identified from publicly available transcriptome sequencing data. SSCVs are variants that may create new splice sites and potentially cause aberrant splicing. In TogoVar, the predicted splicing type registered in SSCV DB is shown with a link to the corresponding SSCV DB record.

Reference:
Iida N, Okada A, Kobayashi Y, Chiba K, Yatabe Y, Shiraishi Y. Systematically developing a registry of splice-site creating variants utilizing massive publicly available transcriptome sequence data. Nat Commun. 2025;16:426. DOI: 10.1038/s41467-024-55185-y. PMID: 39788962.

What is the difference between alternate allele frequency and genotype counts?

In the VCF specification, AC (allele count) is the number of copies of each ALT allele in called genotypes, AF (allele frequency) is its frequency, and AN is the total number of called alleles. Genotype counts are the numbers of individuals with each genotype, such as Ref/Ref, Ref/Alt, or Alt/Alt. In other words, AF and AC are measured at the allele level, whereas genotype counts are measured at the individual level.

For introductory explanations, see the NHGRI definition of allele and the NHGRI definition of genotype.

Q: Can I search byHGVS description(e.g. ALDH2:p.Glu504Lys)?

Yes. HGVSc (e.g.ALDH2:c.1510G>A) and HGVSp (e.g.ALDH2:p.Glu504Lys) are acceptable. Variant Recoderis used to convert an HGVS description to a GRCh38/GRCh37 position.

Q: What can I do with the Advanced search?

You can build a query with an alternate allele frequency, clinical significance, and other search criteria combined with the AND and/or OR operator. See"Q: Can I search for variants with higher alternate allele frequencies in Japanese populations than in other populations?" for usage.

Can I search for variants with higher alternate allele frequencies in Japanese populations than in other populations?

Yes. UseAdvanced search.

The following example uses the GRCh37 version of the site (grch37.togovar.org). ToMMo 8.3KJPN is a dataset included in the GRCh37 version.

Variant sets for comparison of alternate allele frequencies are as follows:

  • For Japanese populations, variants included in either GEM-J WGA or ToMMo 8.3KJPN
  • For other populations, variants included in either gnomAD genomes or gnomAD exomes

This example searches for variants with an alternate allele frequency of at least 0.3 in Japanese populations and at most 0.05 in gnomAD populations.

  1. Select the Advanced search tab at the top of the GRCh37 home page
  2. Select Alternate allele frequency/count from the Add condition menu.

  3. Specify GEM-J WGA and an alternate allele frequency of at least 0.3. You can also specify the number of alternate alleles.
  4. Click OK to set the search condition.
  5. In the same way, set conditions for ToMMo 8.3KJPN, gnomAD genomes, and gnomAD exomes.
  6. Combine the Japanese population conditions for GEM-J WGA and ToMMo 8.3KJPN with OR.
    1. Select the GEM-J WGA and ToMMo 8.3KJPN conditions. Selected conditions are enclosed by yellow borders.
    2. Select the Group menu. The two selected conditions are combined with OR. To change it to AND, click the OR icon on the left.
    3. Combine the gnomAD genomes and gnomAD exomes conditions with OR in the same way.

The final search criteria are as follows:

((freq(GEM-J WGA) >= 0.3) OR (freq(ToMMo 8.3KJPN) >= 0.3))
AND
((freq(gnomAD genomes) <= 0.05) OR (freq(gnomAD exomes) <= 0.05))

Each time search criteria is changed, the search is automatically executed and a list of variants that match the criteria is refreshed.

Functions in the menu bar

  • Add condition: Add search criteria to the query builder.
  • Group: Combine selected search criteria with OR. Click the OR icon to change it to AND.
  • Ungroup: Separate grouped search criteria.
  • Delete: Delete selected search criteria.

Meaning of the icons

  • : Edit search criteria.
  • : Delete search criteria.

Can I search for variants by both positions on GRCh38 and GRCh37?

Yes. The reference assembly is shown in the header. Click it to switch between GRCh37 and GRCh38.
